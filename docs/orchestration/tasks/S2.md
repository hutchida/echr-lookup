# Task S2: Parse + chunk

- **Model:** `github-copilot/claude-sonnet-5.5` (opencode)
- **Database:** `echr_s2` (you barely need it)

## Goal
Turn stored documents into **citable paragraphs** with stable IDs and section labels, and build **three chunking strategies** whose chunks always know which paragraphs they contain. Everything downstream depends on this: citations, search quality and the eval.

## Owned paths
- `py/rag/parse/**`, including its tests in `py/rag/parse/tests/`
- `docs/learn/S2.md`

## Must not touch
Everything else, in particular other `py/rag/*` packages, `py/api/**`, `packages/**` and `data/**`.

## Build (signatures fixed by `docs/interfaces.md` §2 `rag.parse`)

1. **`parse_document(entry, raw) -> {"document", "paragraphs"}`** for every format in the snapshot:
   - HUDOC HTML for judgments, decisions and legal summaries;
   - the Convention text;
   - the HRA 1998 XML from legislation.gov.uk.

   Follow the paragraph-ID conventions in `data/snapshot/README.md` exactly: `<doc>§<n>`, `§op<n>`, `§u<n>`, `§art<n>`. Handle the quirks G0b documented:
   - numbered paragraphs that continue in unnumbered `<p>`s (Soering's §111);
   - glued paragraphs and dot-less numbers;
   - numbering that restarts in operative provisions and separate opinions;
   - unnumbered documents.

   Assign each paragraph a `section` (Procedure / The Facts / The Law / Operative provisions / Other) from the document's headings. IDs must be **deterministic**: the same input gives the same IDs every run.
2. **Inadmissibility grounds from text.** For decisions whose `outcome` is `inadmissible` or `partly_inadmissible` and whose `inadmissibility_grounds` is null, extract the grounds (the `InadmissibilityGround` enum) from the decision text and set `inadmissibility_grounds_source="text"`. Typical phrases: "manifestly ill-founded … Article 35 §§ 3 (a) and 4", "non-exhaustion of domestic remedies", "out of time / six-month (four-month) rule", "incompatible ratione …". `data/snapshot/README.md` has a 35-decision **oracle** from G0b's regex prototype. Aim for ≥ 90% exact-set agreement, and list every disagreement in your learning doc with who you think is right.
3. **`chunk_paragraphs(paragraphs, strategy, count_tokens=None, max_tokens=256)`**:
   - **`fixed`:** sliding window of `max_tokens` with 32 overlap over the document's token stream, ignoring paragraph boundaries; `paragraph_ids` = every paragraph the window touches.
   - **`recursive`:** pack whole paragraphs up to `max_tokens`; split an over-long paragraph on sentences, then on tokens.
   - **`by_section`:** like recursive, but never crosses a section boundary, with a limit of 480 tokens.

   Chunk IDs are `<doc>:<strategy>:<index>`. `paragraph_ids` must be non-empty and in order. `token_count` comes from `count_tokens`, which defaults to a cheap approximation so tests need no ML packages. These defaults come from G0b's benchmark (`docs/learn/ingestion-estimates.md`).
4. **A small CLI for Dan's try-its:** `uv run python -m rag.parse <item_id or path> [--para 88] [--chunks recursive] [--summary]`. It prints the paragraph(s), a section summary or chunk boundaries.

## Acceptance (in addition to the common rules)
- Every snapshot document parses with no exceptions.
- Paragraph and Chunk dicts validate against the contract (test).
- For every judgment, numbered paragraphs have no gaps or duplicates, except documented, tested exceptions.
- Spot tests: Soering `001-57619§88` starts "Article 3 (art. 3) makes no provision for exceptions"; Soering §111 includes its continuation text.
- At least 5 more hand-picked assertions across a decision, a legal summary, the Convention and the HRA.
- Determinism test: parsing twice gives identical output.
- Grounds: ≥ 90% agreement with the oracle (test reports the rate).
- Chunks: no chunk exceeds its limit under the approximation. Add an `@pytest.mark.ml` test using the real bge-small tokenizer: no chunk exceeds 512 tokens.
- Time budgets: parsing + chunking (all 3 strategies) of the whole snapshot takes **< 15 s** with the approximate counter, and **< 45 s** with the real tokenizer. Report both numbers.

### Project context
`echr-lookup` is a learning and portfolio RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) system over the UK's case law at the European Court of Human Rights (HUDOC), plus the Convention and the Human Rights Act 1998.
